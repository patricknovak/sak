The scoring

The bold call collects the most.

Every answer has a price, and the price is the group's chance it happens: 25% means the group thinks one in four. Goblets on an answer at 25% come back about four for one if it comes in, so a call few people agreed with collects more than an obvious one.

The chance moves as friends call the other way, and a player can take Goblets back off a question before it closes. When the last question settles, the player worth the most takes The Golden Goblet. Goblets are this pool's name for its Supercoins: never bought, never sold, and the score is all they measure.

Questions

Before you ask.

Is this a real pool?

No. It is an illustration of a Love Is Blind pool: the questions, the calls and the table. The names are made up, the settled answers are made up, and nothing on this page is read from a live pool.

Is it money?

No. Supercoins, whatever a pool calls them, are never bought, sold or cashed out, and there are no prizes of value.

Who decides the right answer?

The pool's host, by the rule written on the question, with a note everyone sees. A question that something on air makes unclear is voided and every Supercoin goes back.

Do my friends need an app?

No. They tap the link, type a name and they are in on that phone. An email later keeps the seat on another.
