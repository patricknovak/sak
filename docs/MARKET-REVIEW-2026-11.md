## 0. The position in two sentences

Super Pools is the home for every pool a group plays, fantasy leagues for the sports season and prediction pools for anything the group watches, with the chat, a named voice and Supercoins built in and never any money. It was built by a hockey keeper league that has run on it every night since the 2026-27 keeper deadline, and it is the only product in this research that puts a moving price, a host who settles, a live night and the group's own chat in one place with nothing to buy and nothing to win but the crown (PI §0; market-pools.md §5; market-prediction.md §4; market-shows.md §8).

One exception to BRAND §4, already on the live landing (`landing/index.html` lines 46, 414, 488, 536): the import sources may be named as sources ("rosters and history from Yahoo, ESPN and Fantrax"), never as comparisons; nothing else on a public page names a competitor.

## 3. The ten differentiators true today

Ranked by how much each would move a stranger. Struck because the incumbents now have them (market-fantasy.md §3.2, §5): the guillotine (ESPN Knockout, Sleeper Chopped, MFL Chop), badges and record books (ESPN, Yahoo), dues wallets (SleeperSafe, Fantrax Treasurer), league presets and free commissioner tools.

1. Never money: Supercoins never bought, sold or cashed out, no ads, no rake (`#/terms`, migration 145; PI §5, §6). The pool hosts' and the markets' complaint surface is money: Splash's rake (by its own former FAQ) and withdrawals, RunYourPool's fee after round one, Kalshi's 39 states, the NHL's own loot boxes (market-pools.md §3.1, §4.4; market-prediction.md §1; market-fantasy.md §2.6). The fantasy incumbents' is crashes, ads and redesigns (market-fantasy.md §2), which no-money does not answer and a night that holds does.
2. Twelve kinds of pool built on one engine, seven open today (pick'em, last one standing, Call the score, the streak, the box pool, prop sheets and squares on the NFL) and the rest opening with the playoffs (series, rank, the bracket, the sweepstake; win totals before next season), and a fantasy league, one account, one scoreboard (migration 297; `my_pools()`, migration 151; PI §2.1, §5, §8). No host runs more than five or six kinds. Sleeper and Yahoo put pick'em and survivor pools beside their football leagues (market-pools.md §3.7, §3.11), but none puts a pool and a fantasy league on one scoreboard, one account and one coin for the same group, and none of them runs a hockey league (market-pools.md §5).
3. A price that moves as friends call it, with no money behind it (migrations 145 to 147; PI §3). Real-money exchanges have the price, and Manifold has it with play money on a public site of strangers (market-prediction.md §3.1); no private friends' pool has it, and nobody puts it beside a chat, a host who settles and a record across seasons (market-prediction.md §4; market-shows.md §5).
4. A night, not a leaderboard. In the fantasy league every player locks at his own puck drop and the score moves on a 20-second clock (migrations 246 to 248; PI §1); in every pool the table shows max possible and who can't catch first as the games end (`_pool_rows()`; PI §2.1). The market's live scoring is a static table; OfficePools' reviews say "not real-time" (market-pools.md §4.3; market-hockey.md §7.1).
5. The chat is the product: one feed, a thread per game, fourteen lounges, live rooms, photos, polls (migrations 310, 313, 315, 333, 265; PI §5). Every host sends the invite into someone else's chat (market-pools.md §4.2).
6. A named voice with a byline that posts only when something happened (`garry`, `poolDaily`, migrations 65, 99, 243, 264; PI §1, §3). Its columns, nudges and answers are checked against the league's table before they post; a line the facts don't hold is cut or the post is dropped (`numbersCheck` and `factCheck` in `supabase/functions/garry/index.ts`; `#/terms` says the voice is a language model and can be wrong). No incumbent ships a named voice in the league's chat. Yahoo's paid Plus tools reach hockey but Yahoo does not call them AI, and ESPN's IBM grades are football only, so say "the only league voice in fantasy hockey we know of" (market-ai.md §2, §5.7, §8).
7. In with just a name: the invite asks the first question and the call lands the moment the guest is in (migrations 252, 255, 332; PI §3). "One link in the group chat, in before the pods open, is still unmatched" (market-shows.md §8).
8. Keeper rules built by a keeper league: keepers, the draft room, trade review, IR rules, imports (PI §1). Yahoo's keeper is a slot it cannot enforce, ESPN's a round swap; Sleeper has keeper and dynasty leagues but no hockey at all (market-fantasy.md §2, §2.3; market-hockey.md §5).
9. The host settles by the rule written on the question, with the reason posted to chat, or voids the question and every coin goes back to whoever put it on; the pool bells the host if it waits (migration 254; PI §3). A host can settle the same night; show pools wait 2 to 72 hours for a central scorer (market-shows.md §8).
10. Fan zones for every club in four leagues, open with no account, open data, MIT (migrations 337 to 339; PI §4). A reason to open the app on a day with no pick, which no pool host offers (market-pools.md §5).

## 5. The offering

The permanent rules stand: no money in the product, Supercoins never bought or sold, no ads, no rake, no prizes of value (BRAND §2; MARKET §5; market-prediction.md §5.3).

**Free forever, written as a promise** (pricing.md §2.2): a member never pays; every kind of pool is free for a friends-sized group with Supercoins, chat, the live room, sport centres and fan zones; a league's history stays readable whether or not it ever paid (no league export exists today, PI §1; the word "exportable" enters the promise only in the pull request that adds one, a CSV of standings, rosters and transactions per season from `#/league`); bringing a league in is free; no ads on any tier; no money in the product.

**The planned ladder, per pool per season** (nothing is charged or gated today, PI §7.1; pricing.md §2.1; MARKET §5):

| Tier | Price | Unit | Adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | per pool | every pool kind up to 30 entries, a presets fantasy league up to 12 teams, chat, Supercoins, imports |
| Plus | $49 a season; $19 for an event under 30 days | per pool per season | custom scoring, keepers, co-hosts, constitution, audit trail, dues tracker, pools above 30 entries, a weekly column from the voice |
| Premium | $99 a season (hold until `meter_cost` shows the voice's cost per league; $119 if high) | per league per season | the full voice, the Book in Supercoins, projections; contracts, caps and prospects marked coming until they ship (PI §7.7) |
| The Book | $19 a season, in Premium | per pool per season | head-to-heads and props between GMs and the league's own lines on NHL games, all in Supercoins, settled from the box scores; nothing to buy, nothing to cash out |
| Super Pool bundle | $149 a year | per crew per year | Premium in every pool the group runs |

Until billing ships, every pool has every row; on the site, the three pricing cards carry the same line. Every tier is a fee for the software; no tier sells coins or anything coins can win (MARKET §3 rule 1, §5 rule 1).

Show our own per-head figure small beside the price ($99 is $8.25 a team in a 12-team league); the comparison is internal only: $480 to $960 for twelve people on Yahoo's Plus and Ultra at the table's App Store prices (pricing.md §1.2 counts $420 from a $34.99 in-app snapshot; one source, the table's, is used here). Say "season fee", never "subscription" or "entry fee"; no auto-renew; a pool that does not play does not pay. Do not say Premium "undercuts Fantrax" anywhere until Fantrax's NHL web price is confirmed (pricing.md §3).

**The founding season** (pricing.md §2.3; nothing is gated today, PI §7.1): every pool that starts in 2026-27 plays with every feature free until its season ends (June 2027 for the NHL, the Super Bowl for the NFL, the finale for a show). Founding pools get a founding rate, 30% under list, for as long as they renew without a gap: Plus $34, Premium $69, bundle $104, published now beside the list prices with a "Planned" pill. List prices apply to pools that start on or after 1 September 2027, before the 2027-28 hockey and football pools form (market-hockey.md §10); founding pools are told the final list by 1 July 2027. A "Reserve the founding rate" button (email, no card), a founders' mark on the pool page and the first vote on the feature board. No beta label, no cards; the promise enters `#/terms` in the pull request that ships billing.

**Five objections, answered** (pricing.md §2.5). The internal answer names the rival; the public version, for `/pricing`, names none (BRAND §4) and shows only our own per-head figure beside the price.

1. "ESPN, Yahoo and Sleeper are free." Internal: paid for with ads and their rules; Yahoo sells "fewer ads" for $79.99 a person; twelve people on Yahoo's tiers pay $480 to $960. Public: "The big free sites are paid for with ads and their own rules, and one now sells 'fewer ads' per person, up to $80 a head a year. Our free pool has no ads, and Premium is $99 for the whole league, $8.25 a team."
2. "Why should I pay for everyone?" The price is per pool, one payment covers the league, anyone can chip in a share, and members never see a bill.
3. "What if you disappear?" Internal: RotoWire closed its commissioner service this year with a download link on its last day. Public: "Your history is readable on every tier, every day, not only on the day a service closes." The word "exportable" joins this line in the pull request that ships the export (above).
4. "We already pay dues; this feels like a rake." Public: "A flat season fee for the software, like booking the room. Nothing your league does among yourselves passes through us: no rake, no entry fee to us, no house, Supercoins never bought or sold, and the ledger shows every coin's reason." No dollar figure for a pot anywhere public (`#/terms` keeps a group's own stakes at arm's length; §4 item 13).
5. "The voice is a gimmick." It says only what the box scores and the league's record say; its columns, nudges and answers are checked against the league's table before they post, and a line the facts don't hold is cut or the post is dropped. It has a daily budget, and the commissioner names it and tells it what the league is about (`commish_set_brand`, `garry_budget`, migration 99; PI §1, §6). No off switch exists (`LeagueFeatures.tsx` has no voice entry); if the page is to say "switch it off", a `features.voice` switch ships in the same pull request.
