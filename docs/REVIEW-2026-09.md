# Season-one review: how the site is built, what it costs to run, and where it goes next

Written at the end of opening week (30 Sep 2026). Everything below was checked against the live project, not
from memory.

## 1. What runs, and whether it is working

**Health.** No scheduled job failed in the last 24 hours, every outbound call from the scheduler succeeded
(388 of 388), and the last health alert was 28 Sep (a transient scheduler stall that cleared itself). The
database is 51 MB. Scoring matched the NHL box scores line for line in the audit before this one; the
lineup history added in this change reads the same rows the standings count, so what a GM sees for a played
day is exactly what he was credited.

**Moving parts.**

| Piece | What it does | Runs as |
|---|---|---|
| Postgres (Supabase) | the league: rosters, lineups, scoring views, bets, chat, money; every rule is a SQL function behind row-level security | |
| `nhl-sync` edge function | scores, box scores, lineup freeze-frames, schedule, injuries, game-day status, player news, projections, auto-lineups | cron via pg_net |
| `nhl-hub` edge function | the NHL centre: scores, standings, news, X posts (Grok search), leaders; cached in `hub_cache` | site, on demand |
| `garry` edge function | Garry's posts, answers, grades, recaps; the LLM is Grok (xAI), not Claude | cron + chat triggers |
| `player-info`, `push`, `yahoo` | player pages, web push, Yahoo pool import | site, on demand |
| GitHub Pages | the site itself, static, built by Actions on merge to main | |

## 2. Resource use, and what this change trims

Edge function invocations per day before this change, from the last 24 hours of logs:

| Function | Calls/day | Avg time | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| nhl-sync | ~1,505 | 1.5 s | `nhl-scores` every minute, 24/7 (1,440 of them) |
| nhl-hub | ~490 | 0.3 s | GMs opening the NHL centre and Home; 5-minute shared cache |
| garry | ~13 | 11 s | daily/weekly posts and questions |
| others | ~80 | | player pages, push, Yahoo |

Grok (xAI) spend is only Garry's posts and answers (a dozen calls a day) plus the X feed search, cached for
five minutes and shared by everyone, so at most 288 searches a day and in practice far fewer. Nothing on the
site calls an LLM per page view.

Garry runs on `grok-4.3` (xAI retired grok-4 and the fast models on 15 May 2026; their ids redirect to grok-4.3 at
its price: $1.25 per million input tokens, $0.20 cached, $2.50 output). Chat replies call it with reasoning off and
measured about 2,600 input and 100 output tokens each, roughly $0.003 a reply and 3 seconds; the shared prompt start
(persona, briefing, voice notes) is served from xAI's cache. Unprompted moments add at most two posts a day. Today's
calls and tokens are kept on `garry_state.usage` (reset each Eastern day), so the spend can be read any time.

**Trimmed in this change** (migrations 57 and 58, already applied):

- The minute-by-minute score sync now runs only while a game is live, about to start (the lineup
  freeze-frame is taken at puck drop), or just ended and not yet pulled into the box scores, with a run every
  15 minutes as a backstop. On a game night that is roughly 450 calls instead of 1,440; on an off day about
  100. Expect nhl-sync to drop by two thirds.
- The hourly injury pull now skips the hours the every-15-minutes game-day task already covers (it refreshes
  injuries itself).
- The scheduler's run history (66,000 rows, 15 MB, growing 9,000 rows a day because the trade/draft ticker
  runs every 10 seconds) is pruned weekly to the last 7 days.
- Indexes on the foreign keys the site joins on (messages, bets, tickets, trades, picks, ledgers), and the
  unused name index on players dropped.

**Left as is, on purpose.** The 10-second `process-pending` ticker is a trivial SQL call and is what makes
draft picks and accepted trades feel instant; the every-30-minutes Book settlement and health check are
SQL only. The 5-minute hub cache is the right trade-off between freshness and calls.

### October 2026 cost pass

Measured on 2 October from the live logs and the providers' own usage numbers.

**What the bill is made of.** Supabase is a flat plan: Pro at $25 a month for the organisation, plus about $10 a
month of compute for this project (the plan's compute credit covers one project, and the organisation runs ten).
SaK uses a sliver of what that buys: about 1,700 edge-function calls a day (51,000 a month against 2 million
included), a 58 MB database (8 GB included), and well under a gigabyte of egress a day (250 GB a month included),
so nothing it does today can add an overage. GitHub Pages is free. The only costs that move with use are xAI's:

| What | Measured | Before this pass | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| X insiders feed (Grok X search) | $0.113 for one incremental search: 19 posts read at half a cent each (xAI bills every post the search looks at) plus $0.018 of tokens | two searches per refresh over a two-day window, up to 40 posts each, every 5 minutes anyone was looking: roughly $0.50 a refresh, likely a few hundred dollars a month | one search per refresh, only posts newer than the feed's newest, every 10 minutes from 11 am to 1 am ET and hourly overnight: about $0.10 a refresh, a fifth or less of before |
| Garry | 18 calls, 45k tokens in, 2k out on a busy day: about $0.06 | | unchanged |

Every paid call now lands on the running-costs ledger (`ops.cost_usage`, migration 78) at the price xAI returns with
it, by Eastern day, league and feature, and the fixed bills sit beside it (`ops.cost_fixed`). The platform owner reads
both on the **Running costs** page (`#/costs`, linked from the Commissioner page): month to date and projected,
daily and monthly totals by source, cost per call and per day for each feature, and each league's own spend plus
its share of the shared costs, per GM. Each morning `cost-watch` compares yesterday's AI spend with the week before
and notifies the owner of a spike (more than double the average and at least 50 cents more, or over $5 in a day).
The first metered X search read 10 posts for 6.4 cents.

**Also trimmed:**

- The player sync rewrote all 772 rostered players six times a day and the injury sync rewrote every injured
  player (about 100) every 15 minutes on game days, changed or not: thousands of needless writes a day, and why
  `players` was 17 MB for 1.5 MB of data. Both now write only what changed (first runs: 1 of 772, 0 of 102).
- Every open phone refreshed three standings tables every minute and the season stats every five, all day. With
  no game on or about to start the numbers can't move, so it's every 5 and every 30 minutes then; game nights keep
  the live pace, and game starts arrive by realtime.

**What to do as the product grows (in order of payoff):**

1. One shared feed per sport, never per league: the X feed, NHL centre, news and projections cost the same for one
   league or a thousand. Keep the cache keys per sport (and per account list only when a commissioner changes it).
2. Ship the shared player data as a file, not a query: the site reloads the whole player list (634 KB raw, plus
   385 KB of form windows) about 600 times a day for ten people. A per-sport JSON file rebuilt by the sync when
   something changes (Supabase Storage behind its CDN, cached by the browser) takes that off the database
   entirely and costs nothing more with each league. `players.updated_at` now means something, so a "what
   changed since" read also works.
3. Score once per scoring profile (`docs/EXPANSION.md` B3), and store finished days' standings instead of
   re-summing the season on every read, so database load grows with leagues, not with leagues times days.
4. Garry: a daily call budget per league (by plan tier), and the Batch API (20% off) for the scheduled posts.
5. Host the site and landing page on Cloudflare Pages (free, commercial use allowed, DNS already there) rather
   than Vercel Pro ($20 a month per member); Vercel's free tier doesn't allow commercial use. *Decided (Patrick,
   3 October 2026); the move is in `docs/EXPANSION.md`.*
6. Stay on one Supabase project and grow its compute only when the database needs it; a staging project
   (about $10 a month) before the first paying league is still worth it.

## 3. Security posture after this change

- Every scoring and money view now runs as the caller (`security_invoker`), so the table policies decide
  what a view shows. This closes the linter's "security definer view" errors. The only exception is
  `team_directory`, which is deliberately the public list of team names and colours the login page shows.
- Internal helper functions pin their `search_path` and no longer grant execute to `anon`; the settlement
  and payout helpers are no longer callable by signed-in GMs directly (they run inside security-definer
  functions and the scheduler).
- Still open, and only settable in the Supabase dashboard: leaked-password protection under Auth settings,
  and `pg_net` living in the public schema (harmless; moving it is a Supabase-managed change).
- `rosters_2526_backup` has no primary key. It is a one-off snapshot from before the keeper reset and can
  be dropped once the season is safely under way.
- October 2026 update (migrations 76-77, `docs/EXPANSION.md`): `_payout_market` had become callable with the
  public key again (a later migration recreated it with the default grants; no payout was ever made outside
  the scheduler) and is revoked; `team_directory` carries every GM's `login_email` as well as names and colours,
  so the login emails are readable with the public key (a decision for the sign-in redesign); Garry's
  commissioner tasks now need a commissioner session or the platform admin key; 26 security-definer functions
  check that the id they are given belongs to the caller's league; `supabase/tests/tenancy.sql` fails the test
  run if a new table, view or function breaks these rules.

## 4. Turning it into a product for other leagues

The code already keeps league rules in data (`league` row: roster caps, scoring weights, keepers, IR rules,
pickups, money split), so most of the work is separating leagues from each other, not rewriting rules.

1. **One database, many leagues.** Add `league_id` to every league-scoped table (teams, rosters, picks,
   messages, bets, ledgers, lineup plans and snapshots, transactions) and to every view and function; the
   RLS policies change from "any signed-in user" to "members of this league". Shared tables stay shared:
   players, games, player_games, player_status, player_events, hub_cache. That is the single biggest job and
   it is mechanical.
2. **Shared NHL feed, paid for once.** nhl-sync already writes league-independent data; with many leagues
   the same box score serves all of them. Scoring becomes a per-league view over shared player_games with
   the league's weights, which is how it works today with one league.
3. **Accounts and roles.** Today a team is a login. A product needs a person who can own several teams in
   several leagues, an invite flow, and a commissioner per league with the tools the Commish page has now.
4. **Garry per league.** His memory, tone and posts are already keyed by team and channel; they need a
   league key and a per-league budget (posts per day) because the LLM is the one cost that scales with
   leagues, not with users.
5. **Money.** Coins are internal and fine. Real-money tracking is bookkeeping between friends today; as a
   product it should stay bookkeeping (no payments handled) or be dropped, to avoid gaming regulation.
6. **Branding and domains.** The SaK name, the Peter, the history page and the promo video are this
   league's; they move behind a per-league theme (name, colours, trophy names, rulebook text).
7. **Billing and hosting.** Supabase Pro plus a Cloudflare Pages front end covers hundreds of leagues; a
   Stripe subscription per league per season is the natural price. Sign-up, billing and a landing page are
   new work.
8. **Order of work.** Keep building here through this season; in the spring do step 1 on a branch with the
   test suite (it already covers the full draft-to-settlement flow), then steps 3 and 6, then billing.

## 5. Feature ideas worth building next

Ranked by how much they use what already exists.

- **Weekly report card by push and chat.** Garry already grades trades and drafts; a Monday card per GM
  (points, best and worst start, points left on the bench from the new lineup history, rank moves) is a
  small task over `team_daily` and `lineup_snapshots`.
- **Bench regret meter on the lineup page.** The lineup history now knows what the bench scored every day;
  a season total per GM ("points left on the bench") on the standings and Home would be a talking point.
- **Live game-night pushes.** The gameday task knows who is a confirmed starter; a push at 5 pm ET
  "Oettinger is starting, you have him on the bench" closes the last gap between the data and the GM.
- **Trade finder from needs.** The win-win trade suggestions and roster-needs code exist; a "propose this"
  button that opens the trade form pre-filled would make them usable in two taps.
- **Season awards page.** Hat tricks, shutouts, biggest night, longest streak, most points from waivers:
  all derivable from player_games and transactions; nothing to ingest.
- **Yahoo import as onboarding.** The Yahoo connector already reads a pool; for other leagues it becomes
  the way to bring a league in with rosters and history intact.
- **Playoff bracket bets.** The Book settles from box scores; a bracket pool at the NHL playoffs is a new
  market kind, not a new system.
